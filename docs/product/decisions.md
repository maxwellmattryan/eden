## How to use this doc

Cite ids; never restate. To settle an open question, add a `D-n` entry and mark the `OQ-n` "promoted to D-n". To change a decision, add a new `D-n` and mark the old one "superseded by D-n". Both stay in place.

| D-73 | The scheduler, signals and the inbox. **The tick** is the crate's, because a timer in a hidden webview is slowed or stopped: an alarm looks at the earliest schedule every thirty seconds and tells the shell, and changes nothing. The shell takes what is due when the alarm rings, when it starts and when its window comes back, and only a take moves a schedule on. So an occurrence is handed over at most once, never to a webview that was not listening, and catch-up covers a launch, a wake and the return to the foreground alike. A take answers each schedule once however many occurrences were missed. A schedule is `once`, `daily` at a local time or `every` so many seconds, a minute at least; a manifest declares the repeating ones (`schedules`) and a domain sets a one-shot while the app runs. `scheduler.fired` is handed to its subscribers and never stored. **A signal** is a row: a name, a payload of URIs and a few fields (4 KB at most), the tier of what it names, and an optional key under which it is emitted once. **A rule** is a notification kind of a manifest with a `signal` and an optional `when`. The frontend evaluates the rules and works out the tier, as it alone knows the shape of a fact (D-72), and the crate stores the signal with the inbox cards it is handed, as one write. A card carries no words: its line is written from the rule's locale keys and the signal's payload. An OS notification needs the `os-notifications` capability on the device (D-70), which the first card that would have been one asks for, and from T2 up it names the domain and not what happened. Schedules, signals and the inbox are this device's: never exported, never synced, never cleared by a replace; signals and their cards are kept thirty days. **Freshness** is the refresh coordinator's: a mirror is fetched on its short interval while something reads it and the window is seen, when its schedule fires if it has one, and not otherwise, so the scheduler owns the clock for what must happen unobserved and the coordinator owns what is on screen. | D-74 | Model grades. A model is picked by the job, not the place. A job whose answer is numbers Eden has or can fetch is plain; a job of judgement is graded. No tool and no manifest names a model: each provider in the provider registry carries its models, each with its flags and a pricing row, and a map from grade to model, all seeded and the owner's to edit; a provider with one model maps all three to it. A request that would run above its declared grade is confirmed the same way, whatever the two grades are. The Gardener never changes its own grade: it may propose a higher one, or a lower one where the job does not need the power, and the owner decides. Choosing a model that costs more than the seeded one for its grade says by how much and asks first. The conversation has a grade of its own, `standard` until the owner switches it on the status-bar chip; the switch is the owner's, so what follows it runs at that grade without asking again. A plain tool called from a conversation runs inline; a model-backed one is its own single request at its own grade, with a context pack of its declared reads alone, and answers the conversation: bounded, never a loop. An estimate uses the resolved model's pricing row; every request, a delegated one included, writes its own audit entry with its thread, the request that called it, its tool, its declared and resolved grade, how its model was chosen, its provider and model, and the outcome of any confirm, so that every tool call can be walked back to its chat. | D-75 | The Tasks substrate, Phase 1. **Task logic lives in the frontend**, in `@eden/shared/recurrence` and `@eden/shared/tasks`, pure and tested in Node; the crate holds `recurrence`, `target` and `progress` as JSON it does not read, as it holds a fact's value (D-72). **The shapes**: a recurrence is an RRULE subset (`freq`, `start`, `interval`, `weekdays`, `until`, `count`; monthly and yearly skip a month without the day, weekly intervals count Monday weeks whatever the week start), a habit's target is a count per day or week, and progress is a record of days, each done at an instant, skipped, or tallied; what is malformed reads as `null`. **Occurrences are computed, never materialised**: a routine is one row whose `due` stays `null`, its state for a day in `progress`; a habit tallies by day, is measured over its period from the owner's week start (D-58), and its streak is recomputed and written on each tally, with up to `grace` missed periods in a row forgiven. **Snooze moves `due`**: later today (the next full hour plus two, absent once that is tomorrow), tomorrow, or the first day of next week, keeping the time of day; no column is added. **A line added on Today with no date is due today**; a task made elsewhere with no due does not appear. **Overdue** is a todo or a checklist not done whose due day, in the owner's zone, is before today; a timed task past its time today stays under Due today, marked, and a missed routine never becomes overdue. **The task signals are the frontend's** (amends the last sentence of D-73 for `task.created` and `task.completed` only): whatever creates or completes a task for the owner emits through `@eden/shared/tasks` after its write lands, one signal per owner action, the payload the URI, the kind, the day and the title clipped to 200 characters, keyed by the id for a creation and by the id and the day for a completion (the first day of the period when a habit meets its target). A batch (the seed) and an import emit nothing; a failed emit never fails the write; an undo retracts nothing.
